Start with the scope before choosing the stack

Five questions that connect a technology decision with the users, dependencies and operating work behind it.

Begin with the work

A platform discussion is easier to evaluate when the business requirement is clear. Identify the workflow that needs to change, the people who use it and the decision or task the system should support.

Map the dependencies

List the applications, data sources, integrations and access owners involved. Separate what can change in the engagement from what needs another team or vendor. This gives the scope a more useful boundary than a list of technologies alone.

Define how the work will be reviewed

Agree which requirements are essential, who will review the work and what evidence will be used at each review point. For a migration, include testing and rollback considerations. For an application, include the user workflows and acceptance criteria.

Plan who will operate it

A delivery discussion should include documentation, operational ownership and support boundaries. These details help the business compare a defined project with a longer engagement without assuming a service level or support promise.

A brief to bring to the conversation

Share the objective, current environment, dependencies, review criteria and team responsibilities. The details can evolve during discovery; a concise initial brief still makes the starting point clearer.
